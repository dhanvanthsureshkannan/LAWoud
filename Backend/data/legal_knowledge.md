## Filing a First Information Report (FIR)

**Topic:** FIR / criminal complaint
**Keywords:** fir, first information report, police complaint, cognizable offence, file fir
**Law:** Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, Section 173 (previously CrPC Section 154)
**Source:** NALSA | https://nalsa.gov.in/

An FIR is the first step in a criminal case for a cognizable offence (a
serious offence where police can arrest without a warrant and investigate
without prior court permission).

- Anyone with knowledge of a cognizable offence can file an FIR — not just the
  victim.
- It can be given orally or in writing at the police station having
  jurisdiction; an oral report must be written down by the police, read back
  to you, and you should sign it after confirming it is accurate.
- You are entitled to a free copy of the FIR after it is registered.
- If a police station refuses to register an FIR for a cognizable offence, you
  can approach the Superintendent of Police, or a Magistrate under the
  relevant provision of the BNSS.
- For non-cognizable offences, the police will guide you to file a complaint
  before a Magistrate instead of registering an FIR directly.

## Rights of an Arrested Person

**Topic:** Rights on arrest
**Keywords:** arrest rights, arrested, police custody, bail, legal aid on arrest
**Law:** Constitution of India Article 22; BNSS provisions on arrest
**Source:** NALSA | https://nalsa.gov.in/

A person who is arrested has specific constitutional and statutory
protections:

- The right to be informed of the grounds of arrest as soon as possible.
- The right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner of their
  choice.
- The right to be produced before the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours of
  arrest (excluding travel time), except in specific cases defined by law.
- The right to inform a friend or relative about the arrest and the place of
  detention.
- If a person cannot afford a lawyer, they are entitled to free legal aid
  through the Legal Services Authority.

## Tenant Rights and Eviction

**Topic:** Tenant eviction and rent
**Keywords:** tenant, eviction, landlord, rent, lease, tenancy dispute
**Law:** State-specific Rent Control Acts / Model Tenancy Act, 2021
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Tenancy law in India is largely governed by state-level Rent Control Acts, so
exact procedures vary by state; the Model Tenancy Act, 2021 is a framework
states may adopt.

- A landlord generally cannot evict a tenant without following due legal
  process — this typically requires valid grounds (such as non-payment of
  rent, unauthorized subletting, or the landlord's bona fide personal need)
  and a court or Rent Authority order.
  Self-help eviction (changing locks, cutting utilities, forcibly removing a
  tenant) is not lawful even where grounds for eviction exist.
- Tenants are usually entitled to a written notice period before eviction
  proceedings, as specified in the applicable state Rent Control Act or the
  Model Tenancy Act framework.
- A security deposit is expected to be refunded at the end of the tenancy,
  subject to lawful deductions for damages, under the terms of the tenancy
  agreement and applicable state law.
- Disputes are generally handled by Rent Courts / Rent Authorities or Civil
  Courts, depending on the state.

## Cheque Bounce (Dishonour of Cheque)

**Topic:** Cheque bounce / dishonour
**Keywords:** cheque bounce, dishonour of cheque, section 138, negotiable instruments act
**Law:** Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, Section 138
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 makes dishonour of a
cheque (e.g. due to insufficient funds) a criminal offence, in addition to
being a civil matter.

- The payee must present the cheque within its validity period.
- After dishonour, the payee must send a written demand notice to the drawer
  within 30 days of receiving the "cheque return memo" from the bank,
  demanding payment within 15 days of the notice.
- If the drawer fails to pay within that 15-day period, the payee can file a
  criminal complaint under Section 138 within one month of the cause of
  action arising.
- Both a criminal complaint under Section 138 and a civil recovery suit are
  legally possible remedies.

## Consumer Complaints

**Topic:** Consumer protection and complaints
**Keywords:** consumer complaint, defective product, refund, consumer forum, deficiency in service
**Law:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019
**Source:** Department of Consumer Affairs | https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 protects buyers of goods and services
against defective products, deficient services, unfair trade practices, and
misleading advertisements.

- Complaints can be filed at District, State, or National Consumer Disputes
  Redressal Commissions, depending on the value of goods/services and
  compensation claimed.
- Consumers can also file complaints online through the National Consumer
  Helpline / e-Daakhil portal.
- Reliefs available include replacement, refund, compensation for loss or
  injury, and removal of defects or deficiencies.
- There is generally a limitation period of two years from the date the cause
  of action arose to file a consumer complaint.

## Domestic Violence Protection

**Topic:** Domestic violence
**Keywords:** domestic violence, protection order, dowry, harassment, matrimonial home
**Law:** Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005
**Source:** NALSA | https://nalsa.gov.in/

The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 provides civil law
remedies (in addition to any criminal remedies) to women facing physical,
sexual, verbal, emotional, or economic abuse from a person they are or have
been in a domestic relationship with.

- A complaint can be filed with a Protection Officer, service provider, or
  directly before a Magistrate.
- Available reliefs include Protection Orders, Residence Orders (right to
  stay in the shared household), monetary relief, custody orders, and
  compensation orders.
- Free legal aid is available through the Legal Services Authorities for
  eligible complainants.
- The National Commission for Women helpline (7827170170) and NALSA's toll
  free number (15100) provide guidance in such situations.

## Right to Information (RTI)

**Topic:** RTI applications
**Keywords:** rti, right to information, public authority, information request
**Law:** Right to Information Act, 2005
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

The Right to Information Act, 2005 lets citizens request information from
public authorities.

- An application can be filed with the Public Information Officer (PIO) of
  the relevant public authority, along with the prescribed fee (fee waived
  for applicants below the poverty line).
- The PIO must respond within 30 days (48 hours if the information concerns
  life or liberty).
- If unsatisfied, the applicant can file a first appeal with the departmental
  appellate authority, and thereafter a second appeal with the State/Central
  Information Commission.

## Cybercrime and Online Fraud

**Topic:** Cybercrime reporting
**Keywords:** cybercrime, online fraud, phishing, otp fraud, hacking, cyber complaint
**Law:** Information Technology Act, 2000
**Source:** National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal | https://cybercrime.gov.in/

Cyber offences such as online financial fraud, hacking, identity theft, and
online harassment can be reported through the National Cyber Crime Reporting
Portal.

- Financial fraud should be reported immediately — the national cyber
  helpline (1930) can help initiate a request to freeze fraudulent
  transactions if reported quickly.
- Complaints can be filed online without needing to visit a police station
  first, though a police FIR may still be needed depending on the case.
- Evidence such as screenshots, transaction IDs, and correspondence should be
  preserved before reporting.

## Motor Vehicle Accident Claims

**Topic:** Motor accident compensation
**Keywords:** motor accident, accident claim, insurance claim, motor vehicles act, compensation
**Law:** Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (as amended)
**Source:** India Code | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/

Victims of motor vehicle accidents (or their legal heirs, in case of death)
can claim compensation through a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal (MACT).

- A claim petition can generally be filed regardless of who was at fault, and
  there is generally no fixed limitation period for filing such claims,
  though filing promptly is advisable.
- Required documents typically include the FIR, medical records/post-mortem
  report, proof of income, and vehicle/insurance details.
- Compensation may cover medical expenses, loss of income, and in fatal
  cases, loss of dependency to the family.

## Legal Aid and Free Legal Services

**Topic:** Free legal aid eligibility
**Keywords:** free legal aid, legal services authority, nalsa, eligibility, pro bono lawyer
**Law:** Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987
**Source:** NALSA | https://nalsa.gov.in/

Free legal aid is available under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 to
categories including:

- Women and children
- Members of Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes
- Persons with disabilities
- Industrial workmen
- Victims of trafficking or a mass disaster, violence, flood, drought,
  earthquake, or industrial disaster
- Persons in custody
- Persons with an annual income below the limit specified by the relevant
  State/Central Legal Services Authority

Legal aid can be requested through the National, State, or District Legal
Services Authority, or via the Tele-Law and NyayaBandhu platforms for remote
consultation.
